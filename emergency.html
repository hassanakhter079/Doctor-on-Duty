ACLS Cardiac Arrest Algorithm & Resuscitation

Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support (ACLS) algorithmic management of adult cardiac arrest; shockable (VF / pVT) vs non-shockable (PEA / Asystole) pathways, high-quality chest compressions, early defibrillation, adrenaline & amiodarone protocols, and reversing the 5Hs and 5Ts.

1. The Core Resuscitation Pathways

Survival in sudden cardiac arrest hinges upon immediate recognition, high-quality cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), and rapid rhythm differentiation into shockable vs non-shockable rhythms:

Rhythm CategoryECG RhythmsImmediate Energy & DefibrillationDrug Therapy Pipeline
Shockable (VF / pVT) Ventricular Fibrillation (VF) or Pulseless Ventricular Tachycardia (pVT). Immediate unsynchronized shock: Biphasic: 120–200 Joules (or maximum device output); Monophasic: 360 Joules. Resume CPR immediately for 2 minutes without checking pulse! • Adrenaline (Epinephrine 1:10,000): 1 mg IV/IO after 2nd shock, repeat every 3 to 5 minutes.
• Amiodarone: First dose 300 mg IV/IO bolus after 3rd shock; second dose 150 mg IV/IO after 5th shock.
(Alternative: Lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg IV).
Non-Shockable (PEA / Asystole) Asystole (flatline confirmed in ≥ 2 leads) or Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA) (organized rhythm without palpable pulse). DO NOT SHOCK! Defibrillation is completely futile and causes myocardial damage. Focus 100% on uninterrupted CPR and reversible causes. • Adrenaline (Epinephrine): 1 mg IV/IO as early as possible, then repeat every 3 to 5 minutes.
• Identify and treat underlying 5Hs and 5Ts aggressively.

2. High-Quality CPR Metrics

Rate 100 to 120 Compressions/Minute: Fast, rhythmic compression on lower half of sternum.
Depth At Least 5 to 6 cm (2 to 2.4 inches): In adults, avoiding excessive depths (> 6 cm).
Recoil Full Chest Recoil: Allow the chest to re-expand completely between compressions to allow ventricular filling. Do not lean on the chest!
Min. Pauses Minimize Interruptions: Keep pauses for rhythm checks and shock delivery strictly < 10 seconds. Switch compressors every 2 minutes to prevent fatigue.

3. Reversible Etiologies: The 5Hs and 5Ts

Hypovolemia Rapid IV crystalloid boluses (Ringer's lactate) or O-negative uncrossed blood. Tension Pneumothorax Immediate needle thoracocentesis (2nd ICS MCL or 5th ICS AAL) followed by chest tube.
Hypoxia 100% FiO2 via bag-valve-mask, secure endotracheal airway. Tamponade (Cardiac) Emergent ultrasound-guided pericardiocentesis.
Hydrogen Ion (Acidosis) Adequate ventilation; IV Sodium Bicarbonate (8.4% 50–100 mmol) in prolonged arrest. Toxins (Poisoning) Specific antidotes (Naloxone for opioids, Atropine for organophosphates, Calcium/DigiFab).

Anaphylaxis Emergency Management & Adrenaline Protocol

Severe, sudden-onset multi-system type-I hypersensitivity reaction; airway compromise, bronchospasm, distributive shock, universal first-line Intramuscular Adrenaline (Epinephrine 1:1,000) 0.5 mg anterolateral thigh dosing, aggressive IV fluid resuscitation, and biphasic reaction prevention.

1. Diagnostic Criteria & Triggers

Anaphylaxis is a clinical diagnosis. Treatment must NEVER be delayed for laboratory tests or extensive history:

Trigger Common Triggers: Drugs (Penicillins, Cephalosporins, NSAIDs, IV radiocontrast), food (peanuts, seafood, eggs, milk), insect venoms (wasp/bee stings), latex.
Airway / Resp Respiratory Compromise: Laryngeal edema, stridor, hoarseness, globus sensation, severe bronchospasm, diffuse wheezing, intercostal retraction, SpO2 < 92%.
Cardiovascular Hypotension / Collapse: Systolic BP < 90 mmHg or > 30% drop from baseline, reflex tachycardia, diaphoresis, syncope, cardiac arrest.
Cutaneous Skin / Mucosal Signs: Generalized pruritic urticaria (hives), widespread erythema, angioedema (swollen lips, tongue, uvula, periorbital tissues). Note: Up to 20% of fatal cases lack cutaneous signs due to sudden vascular collapse!

2. The Life-Saving Golden Drug: Intramuscular Adrenaline

ADRENALINE (EPINEPHRINE) IS THE FIRST AND ONLY LIFE-SAVING DRUG:

Antihistamines and steroids do NOT save lives in acute anaphylaxis; they do not reverse bronchospasm or distributive shock. Intramuscular injection into the anterolateral aspect of the middle third of the thigh (vastus lateralis) achieves peak plasma concentrations within 8 minutes (vastly superior to deltoid or subcutaneous routes):

Adult Dose Adults (> 12 years):
0.5 mg IM (0.5 mL of 1:1,000 [1 mg/mL] solution) stat into mid-outer thigh. Repeat every 5 to 15 minutes if symptoms persist or deteriorate!
Child 6–12y Children 6 to 12 years:
0.3 mg IM (0.3 mL of 1:1,000 solution) stat into mid-outer thigh.
Child <6y Children < 6 years:
0.15 mg IM (0.15 mL of 1:1,000 solution) stat into mid-outer thigh.

Sepsis & Septic Shock (Surviving Sepsis Hour-1 Bundle)

Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection (Sepsis-3); bedside qSOFA screening, Surviving Sepsis Campaign Hour-1 Bundle, blood cultures, 30 mL/kg crystalloid bolus, early Norepinephrine vasopressor support (MAP >= 65 mmHg), and empiric broad-spectrum IV antimicrobials.

1. Sepsis-3 Definitions & Bedside qSOFA Score

According to international consensus (Sepsis-3), Sepsis is defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection (quantified by an acute increase in SOFA score ≥ 2). Septic Shock is a subset with persistent hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥ 65 mmHg AND serum lactate > 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation (hospital mortality > 40%):

qSOFA Criteria Quick SOFA Bedside Screen (≥ 2 of 3 indicates high mortality risk):
• Respiratory Rate: ≥ 22 breaths/min.
• Altered Mentation: Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) < 15.
• Systolic Blood Pressure: ≤ 100 mmHg.
Septic Shock Signs Clinical Shock Markers: Mottled skin, capillary refill time > 3 seconds, oliguria (< 0.5 mL/kg/hr), bounding pulses progressing to thready tachycardia, cool extremities, hyperlactatemia.

2. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign "Hour-1 Bundle"

EMERGENCY ACTIONS TO INITIATE WITHIN THE FIRST 60 MINUTES:
Element 1 Measure Serum Lactate: Immediate point-of-care venous/arterial lactate. Re-measure within 2–4 hours if initial lactate is > 2.0 mmol/L to guide resuscitation.
Element 2 Obtain Blood Cultures: Draw 2 sets of peripheral blood cultures (aerobic & anaerobic) from separate venipuncture sites prior to starting antibiotics (without delaying antibiotic initiation > 45 minutes).
Element 3 Administer Broad-Spectrum IV Antimicrobials: Infuse empiric IV antibiotics targeting likely source within the first hour. Mortality increases by 7.6% for every single hour of delay in septic shock!
Element 4 Rapid IV Crystalloid Bolus (30 mL/kg): Administer 30 mL/kg of balanced crystalloid (Ringer's Lactate / Plasmalyte) IV within the first 3 hours for hypotension (MAP < 65) or lactate ≥ 4.0 mmol/L. (Average 70 kg adult = 2,100 mL).
Element 5 Apply Vasopressors to Maintain MAP ≥ 65 mmHg: If blood pressure does not respond during or after fluid loading, start vasopressors immediately to restore perfusion.

Organophosphate & Carbamate Poisoning (Atropinization)

Irreversible acetylcholinesterase inhibition leading to massive acetylcholine storm; DUMBELS / SLUDGEM cholinergic toxidrome, lethal bronchorrhea and bronchospasm, aggressive emergency Atropine titration until target atropinization endpoints, Pralidoxime (PAM) enzyme reactivator, and decontamination.

1. Mechanism & The DUMBELS Cholinergic Toxidrome

Organophosphates (OP: Chlorpyrifos, Malathion, Diazinon, Parathion) and Carbamates are agricultural pesticides widely accessible in Pakistan, frequently ingested for intentional self-harm or absorbed transcutaneously during spraying. They phosphorylate and inactivate acetylcholinesterase (AChE), causing uncontrolled accumulation of acetylcholine at muscarinic, nicotinic, and CNS synapses:

Synaptic ReceptorClinical Manifestations (Toxidrome)Immediate Cause of Death
Muscarinic Receptors (Parasympathetic Overdrive) Mnemonic DUMBELS:
• Diarrhea & Abdominal Cramps
• Urination (Incontinence)
• Miosis (Pinpoint non-reactive pupils)
• Bradycardia, Bronchospasm, Bronchorrhea (Copious frothy pulmonary secretions)
• Emesis (Vomiting)
• Lacrimation
• Salivation & Sweating (Diaphoresis)		 "Drowning in secretions": Massive pulmonary bronchorrhea, intense bronchospasm, and central respiratory drive depression cause fatal asphyxia within hours!
Nicotinic Receptors (Neuromuscular Junction) Mnemonic MTWTF:
• Mydriasis (early paradoxical)
• Tachycardia
• Weakness & Flaccid paralysis
• Tremors
• Fasciculations (Tongue, facial, and diffuse muscle twitches; pathognomonic). Respiratory muscle diaphragm paralysis.
Central Nervous System (CNS) Agitation, confusion, coma, seizures, central respiratory arrest.

2. The Emergency Atropine Titration Protocol

ATROPINE IS THE PHYSIOLOGICAL ANTIDOTE:

Atropine competitively blocks muscarinic receptors, clearing fatal bronchorrhea and reversing bronchospasm. Pupil size and heart rate alone are unreliable markers; the primary goal is drying lung secretions:

Step 1 Initial Bolus: Administer Atropine 1.8 to 3.0 mg IV stat (3 to 5 ampoules of 0.6 mg).
Step 2 The Doubling Rule (Every 5 Minutes): If secretions remain moist and lungs rattling after 5 minutes, double the previous dose (e.g. 3 mg → 6 mg → 12 mg → 24 mg IV) every 5 minutes until complete atropinization is achieved!
Endpoints Target Endpoints of Atropinization:
(1) Lungs completely clear on auscultation (no crackles/bronchorrhea)
(2) Dry mouth and axillae
(3) Heart Rate > 80 beats/min
(4) Systolic BP > 90 mmHg
(5) Pupils dilated ≥ 4mm (pupils may remain small; do not wait for mydriasis if lungs are dry!).
Maintenance Continuous Atropine Infusion: Once atropinized, calculate 10% to 20% of the total loading dose per hour as a continuous IV infusion in normal saline. Taper cautiously over 24 to 72 hours.

3. Cholinesterase Reactivator & Decontamination

InterventionAgent & RegimenClinical Rationale
Cholinesterase Reactivator (Oxime) Pralidoxime (PAM / 2-PAM): 1 to 2 g IV in 100 mL Normal Saline over 30 minutes, followed by 500 mg/hr infusion (or 1g q6h). Cleaves the organophosphate-enzyme bond, regenerating active AChE at nicotinic neuromuscular junctions (reverses muscle weakness and fasciculations). Must be given before "aging" (irreversible covalent bonding, occurs within 24–48 hours).
Seizure Control Diazepam (Valium): 5 to 10 mg slow IV bolus, repeat q10-15min. Suppresses central cholinergic seizures and mitigates excitotoxic neuronal injury.
Dermal Decontamination Strip all clothes, place in sealed bag, wash body with soap and water twice. Prevents continuous transdermal absorption. Healthcare providers must wear nitrile gloves and aprons (secondary poisoning risk).
Diagnostic / Clinical Imaging
Organophosphate Pesticide Toxicity Agricultural pesticide exposure leading to acute cholinergic muscarinic receptor hyper-stimulation.

Paracetamol Toxicity & N-Acetylcysteine (NAC Protocol)

Dose-dependent toxic metabolite (NAPQI) accumulation depleting hepatic glutathione; Rumack-Matthew treatment nomogram, 4 clinical stages, and the definitive intravenous N-Acetylcysteine (NAC / Parvolex) 3-bag protocol preventing fulminant hepatic necrosis.

1. Pathophysiology & Toxic Ingestion Thresholds

Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) is safe at therapeutic doses (max 4 g/day in adults). Metabolism occurs primarily via glucuronidation and sulfation. A small fraction (5–10%) is metabolized by CYP2E1 into the highly reactive, electrophilic, toxic intermediate N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI), normally neutralized by hepatic glutathione:

Toxicity Threshold Doses:
• Acute single ingestion ≥ 150 mg/kg in children or ≥ 7.5 g (15 tablets of 500mg) in adults causes severe hepatic necrosis.
• Ingestion > 250 mg/kg (or > 12 g) carries a high risk of fatal fulminant hepatic failure.
Glutathione Glutathione Depletion: Once hepatic glutathione stores are depleted by > 70%, free uncapped NAPQI binds covalently to cysteinyl sulfhydryl groups of hepatocyte proteins, causing centrilobular (Zone III) hepatic necrosis.

2. The Four Clinical Stages of Poisoning

StageTime Post-IngestionClinical Features & Laboratory Markers
Stage I (Pre-injury) 0.5 to 24 hours Asymptomatic or mild non-specific symptoms: anorexia, nausea, vomiting, malaise, diaphoresis. Normal LFTs.
Stage II (Hepatic Injury) 24 to 72 hours "Quiescent period": Subjective gastrointestinal symptoms improve, but right upper quadrant (RUQ) abdominal pain and liver tenderness appear. AST/ALT and Bilirubin begin to rise sharply; INR/PT prolongs.
Stage III (Peak Toxicity) 72 to 96 hours Peak fulminant hepatic failure: Jaundice, coagulopathy (INR > 5.0), hypoglycemia, hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis, coma), metabolic lactic acidosis, acute renal failure (hepatorenal syndrome / tubular necrosis). AST/ALT frequently > 10,000 U/L. Highest mortality.
Stage IV (Recovery) 4 days to 2 weeks Survivors experience complete histological liver regeneration without permanent cirrhosis or fibrosis.

3. N-Acetylcysteine (NAC) 3-Bag IV Infusion Protocol

N-ACETYLCYSTEINE (NAC / PARVOLEX) IS 100% PROTECTIVE WITHIN 8 HOURS:

NAC replenishes hepatic glutathione and acts as an alternative substrate directly binding NAPQI. If initiated within 8 hours of ingestion, hepatotoxicity risk drops to < 2%:

Infusion BagNAC DosageDiluent Volume (5% Dextrose)Infusion Duration
Bag 1 (Loading Dose) 150 mg/kg 200 mL 5% Dextrose (or 0.9% Saline) Over 1 Hour
Bag 2 (Second Dose) 50 mg/kg 500 mL 5% Dextrose Over 4 Hours
Bag 3 (Third Dose) 100 mg/kg 1,000 mL 5% Dextrose Over 16 Hours

Total Dose: 300 mg/kg IV over 21 hours. (If AST/ALT remains elevated or INR > 1.3 at end of Bag 3, continue Bag 3 rate continuously until LFTs improve).

Diagnostic / Clinical Imaging
Paracetamol Hepatotoxicity Centrilobular Zone-III hepatic necrosis mechanism preventable by early N-acetylcysteine (NAC) infusion.

Snake Bite Envenomation (ASV & 20WBCT Protocol)

Emergency management of venomous snakebites in Pakistan; distinguishing Vasculotoxic/Hemotoxic (Viperidae: Russell's & Saw-scaled viper) from Neurotoxic (Elapidae: Cobra & Krait); the 20-Minute Whole Blood Clotting Test (20WBCT), prohibition of tourniquets, and Polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) titration.

1. Venomous Families in Pakistan

Over 20,000 venomous snakebite envenomations occur annually in Pakistan (especially during monsoons and harvest seasons in Punjab and Sindh):

Venomous FamilyCommon Pakistani SpeciesPathophysiology & Hallmark Signs
Viperidae (Vipers - Vasculotoxic / Hemotoxic) • Russell's Viper (Daboia russelii)
• Saw-scaled Viper (Echis carinatus / لونڈی سانپ)		 Procoagulant venom activates Factor X/prothrombin → Consumption Coagulopathy (DIC), severe local swelling, bleeding from puncture fang marks, spontaneous gum bleeding, hematuria, hemoptysis, and acute renal failure (AKI).
Elapidae (Elapids - Neurotoxic) • Common Krait (Bungarus caeruleus / سنگچور)
• Black Cobra (Naja naja / ناگ)		 Post-synaptic/pre-synaptic neurotoxins block ACh receptors: Bilateral ptosis (earliest sign), ophthalmoplegia, diplopia, bulbar palsy (dysphagia, pooling of secretions), respiratory muscle paralysis. Krait bites frequently occur at night while sleeping with minimal local swelling!

2. The 20-Minute Whole Blood Clotting Test (20WBCT)

Step 1 Draw 2 mL of fresh venous blood into a clean, dry, new glass test tube (never use plastic or tube with anticoagulant/heparin).
Step 2 Leave the tube standing undisturbed at room temperature for exactly 20 minutes.
Result Gently tip the tube to 90°. If blood is still liquid and flows out, 20WBCT is POSITIVE (incoagulable blood), confirming systemic viperid envenomation. Repeat q4h.

3. Dangerous Harmful Practices to Strictly Avoid

  • NO Tourniquets or Tight Bands: Causes ischemic gangrene, limb amputation, and massive toxin surge upon release.
  • NO Incision, Slashing, or Sucking: Causes severe hemorrhage, tendon laceration, and introduces oral bacterial infection.
  • NO Potassium Permanganate, Herbs, or Electric Shocks.
  • Correct First Aid: Reassure patient, immobilize entire bitten limb with a splint/crepe bandage at heart level, and transport immediately to hospital.

4. Polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) Protocol

Initial Dose Standard Loading Dose (Adults & Children receive SAME dose):
Dilute 10 vials of Polyvalent ASV (NIH Islamabad) in 500 mL of 0.9% Normal Saline (100 mL in children). Infuse slowly at 2 mL/min for first 10 minutes (watch for anaphylaxis), then complete over 1 hour.
Monitoring Response Criteria:
• Systemic bleeding stops within 15–30 minutes.
• 20WBCT normalizes (blood clots) within 6 hours. If blood remains incoagulable after 6 hours, repeat full dose of 10 vials of ASV.
Neurotoxic Trial Neostigmine Test (For Cobra/Krait Neurotoxicity):
Give Inj Atropine 0.6mg IV, followed immediately by Inj Neostigmine 1.5mg to 2.5mg IV/IM. If ptosis and respiratory effort improve within 30 minutes, maintain Neostigmine 0.5mg q30min with atropine until recovery.
Diagnostic / Clinical Imaging
Venomous Viperid Snakebite Bitten lower limb displaying severe local edema, hemorrhagic blebs, and 20-Minute Clotting Test failure.

Thermal Burn Injury & Parkland Fluid Resuscitation

Acute major burn resuscitation; Wallace Rule of Nines Total Body Surface Area (TBSA) calculation, Parkland crystalloid formula (4 mL x kg x %TBSA), fluid titration targeting urine output (0.5–1 mL/kg/hr), inhalation injury airway alert, and silver sulfadiazine wound care.

1. TBSA Estimation: The Wallace Rule of Nines

In thermal, flame, and scald burns, accurate calculation of % Total Body Surface Area (% TBSA) of partial-thickness (2nd degree) and full-thickness (3rd degree) burns guides fluid therapy. (Superficial 1st degree erythema is excluded from TBSA calculations!):

Anatomical Body RegionAdult % TBSA (Rule of Nines)Infant / Child < 1 Year
Head & Neck 9% (4.5% anterior, 4.5% posterior) 18% (Head is disproportionately larger)
Anterior Torso (Chest & Abdomen) 18% (9% chest, 9% abdomen) 18%
Posterior Torso (Upper & Lower Back) 18% (9% upper back, 9% buttocks/lower back) 18%
Each Upper Extremity (Right & Left) 9% each (4.5% front, 4.5% back) = 18% total 9% each = 18% total
Each Lower Extremity (Right & Left) 18% each (9% front, 9% back) = 36% total 14% each = 28% total
Perineum & Genitalia 1% 1%
Palmar Surface Rule Patient's entire palm (including fingers) equals approximately 1% TBSA (ideal for patchy scattered burns).

2. The Parkland Fluid Resuscitation Formula

THE PARKLAND (BAXTER) CRYSTALLOID FORMULA:

Indicated for all burns ≥ 15% TBSA in adults (≥ 10% TBSA in children):

Total 24-Hour Fluid (Ringer's Lactate) = 4 mL × Weight (kg) × % TBSA Burn

First 8 Hours Give 50% of Total Volume in First 8 Hours:
Calculated from the exact TIME OF BURN INJURY, NOT the time of hospital arrival!
Next 16 Hours Give Remaining 50% Volume over Next 16 Hours:
Infused at a steady rate over the remaining 16 hours of the first day.

3. Clinical Titration Target: Hourly Urine Output

The Parkland formula is an initial starting estimate. The rate of fluid infusion must be adjusted hourly based on Foley catheter urine output:

  • Adult Target: 0.5 to 1.0 mL/kg/hour (approx 30 to 50 mL/hr).
  • Pediatric Target (< 30 kg): 1.0 to 1.5 mL/kg/hour.
  • Electrical Burns / Rhabdomyolysis / Hemochromogenuria: Target 1.5 to 2.0 mL/kg/hour (75–100 mL/hr) until urine clears to prevent myoglobinuric acute renal tubular necrosis.

4. Inhalation Injury & Topical Wound Care

Inhalation Airway Red Flags (Early Intubation): Singed facial hair/eyebrows, carbonaceous sputum, soot in oropharynx, stridor, voice hoarseness, closed-space fire exposure. Prophylactic endotracheal intubation before laryngeal edema prevents intubation failure!
Wound Care Topical Antimicrobial Dressings:
  • Silver Sulfadiazine 1% cream (Flamazine / Silvadene): Broad-spectrum against Pseudomonas and MRSA. Apply 2–3 mm layer BD. (Avoid near eyes or in sulfa allergy).
  • Polymyxin-Bacitracin Ointment (Polyfax): Preferred for facial burns.

General Approach to the Poisoned Patient (Toxidromes)

Master diagnostic matrix of acute clinical toxidromes (Anticholinergic, Cholinergic, Sympathomimetic, Sedative-Hypnotic, Opioid); emergency decontamination principles, activated charcoal indications, urine alkalinization, and definitive targeted antidotes.

Emergency Acute Pain Management & WHO Ladder

Systematic acute pain assessment using Visual Analog Scale (VAS); WHO 3-step analgesic ladder adapted for acute emergency care, multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia, IV paracetamol/ketorolac protocols, and localized Pakistani analgesics (Provas, Toradol, Tramal, Kinz, Synflex).

1. Pain Assessment & The Visual Analog Scale (VAS)

Pain is the "5th Vital Sign". Rapid, objective pain assessment dictates the immediate analgesic tier:

Mild (1–3) VAS 1 to 3: Mild discomfort, pain is tolerable, does not disrupt conversation or sleep.
Moderate (4–6) VAS 4 to 6: Moderate pain, interferes with movement, concentration, and respiration. Patient is grimacing.
Severe (7–10) VAS 7 to 10: Severe, excruciating, overwhelming pain. Inability to lie still, crying, writhing in agony, tachycardia, diaphoresis.
