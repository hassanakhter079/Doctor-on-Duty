Snake Bite Envenomation (ASV & 20WBCT Protocol)

Emergency management of venomous snakebites in Pakistan; distinguishing Vasculotoxic/Hemotoxic (Viperidae: Russell's & Saw-scaled viper) from Neurotoxic (Elapidae: Cobra & Krait); the 20-Minute Whole Blood Clotting Test (20WBCT), prohibition of tourniquets, and Polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) titration.

Clinical Management & Pharmacotherapy Pakistani Standards

1. Venomous Families in Pakistan

Over 20,000 venomous snakebite envenomations occur annually in Pakistan (especially during monsoons and harvest seasons in Punjab and Sindh):

Venomous FamilyCommon Pakistani SpeciesPathophysiology & Hallmark Signs
Viperidae (Vipers - Vasculotoxic / Hemotoxic) • Russell's Viper (Daboia russelii)
• Saw-scaled Viper (Echis carinatus / لونڈی سانپ)		 Procoagulant venom activates Factor X/prothrombin → Consumption Coagulopathy (DIC), severe local swelling, bleeding from puncture fang marks, spontaneous gum bleeding, hematuria, hemoptysis, and acute renal failure (AKI).
Elapidae (Elapids - Neurotoxic) • Common Krait (Bungarus caeruleus / سنگچور)
• Black Cobra (Naja naja / ناگ)		 Post-synaptic/pre-synaptic neurotoxins block ACh receptors: Bilateral ptosis (earliest sign), ophthalmoplegia, diplopia, bulbar palsy (dysphagia, pooling of secretions), respiratory muscle paralysis. Krait bites frequently occur at night while sleeping with minimal local swelling!

2. The 20-Minute Whole Blood Clotting Test (20WBCT)

Step 1 Draw 2 mL of fresh venous blood into a clean, dry, new glass test tube (never use plastic or tube with anticoagulant/heparin).
Step 2 Leave the tube standing undisturbed at room temperature for exactly 20 minutes.
Result Gently tip the tube to 90°. If blood is still liquid and flows out, 20WBCT is POSITIVE (incoagulable blood), confirming systemic viperid envenomation. Repeat q4h.

3. Dangerous Harmful Practices to Strictly Avoid

  • NO Tourniquets or Tight Bands: Causes ischemic gangrene, limb amputation, and massive toxin surge upon release.
  • NO Incision, Slashing, or Sucking: Causes severe hemorrhage, tendon laceration, and introduces oral bacterial infection.
  • NO Potassium Permanganate, Herbs, or Electric Shocks.
  • Correct First Aid: Reassure patient, immobilize entire bitten limb with a splint/crepe bandage at heart level, and transport immediately to hospital.

4. Polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) Protocol

Initial Dose Standard Loading Dose (Adults & Children receive SAME dose):
Dilute 10 vials of Polyvalent ASV (NIH Islamabad) in 500 mL of 0.9% Normal Saline (100 mL in children). Infuse slowly at 2 mL/min for first 10 minutes (watch for anaphylaxis), then complete over 1 hour.
Monitoring Response Criteria:
• Systemic bleeding stops within 15–30 minutes.
• 20WBCT normalizes (blood clots) within 6 hours. If blood remains incoagulable after 6 hours, repeat full dose of 10 vials of ASV.
Neurotoxic Trial Neostigmine Test (For Cobra/Krait Neurotoxicity):
Give Inj Atropine 0.6mg IV, followed immediately by Inj Neostigmine 1.5mg to 2.5mg IV/IM. If ptosis and respiratory effort improve within 30 minutes, maintain Neostigmine 0.5mg q30min with atropine until recovery.
Clinical Pathology & Radiodiagnostics Verified Visual
Venomous Viperid Snakebite Bitten lower limb displaying severe local edema, hemorrhagic blebs, and 20-Minute Clotting Test failure.
